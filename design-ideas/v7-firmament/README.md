# v7 — Firmament

Today as an open night-black sky over the sea. Built from `v6-hud2/` with the portal ring and its beams taken out. Look only; no app code. There is no reference image for this version.

- The horizon is 376 points down, a little above the middle of the screen, which leaves more than half the screen for the sea and the article titles. The buttons, the panel, `∀`, the sun's path and the titles are all placed from it. The sun and moon travel one low glowing arc, the top segment of a circle whose centre is a little below it (100 points high and 230 wide), set left of centre to leave room for the buttons: its left end is 40 points from the left edge, and its right end is 123 from the right edge, leaving the upper sky open. It rises out of the water at the left and sets into the water at the right, so the path crosses the sea's horizon at both ends. At sunrise, sunset, moon rise and moon set the sun or moon sits half in the water, with its light spilling down the sea below it.
- The sun and moon are larger near the horizon, as they look in life: about twice as wide when rising or setting as when high in the sky, growing and shrinking smoothly along the path.
- The arc keeps v6's light: it thickens and thins along its length, a pulse runs along it, and it is brightest near the sun or moon.
- Stars and thin haze fill the whole sky. They sit on one wheel centred on the middle of the horizon and turn very slowly clockwise (about one turn in 70 minutes), so they rise at the left and set at the right like the sun. They twinkle; they leave no trails.
- Clouds, rain and snow are gathered round the sun or moon and thin out away from it.
- Kept from v6 unchanged: the sun's burning-gas face and the moon's craters and phases, the soft light and film grain, the straight horizon and sea glints, the button column at the right (moved up with the horizon, list still centred on it), the article titles (now six, in the larger sea), and warm accents by day with cool accents at night.
- Header, upper left: month and day, then the day name in a light condensed face (Avenir Next Condensed, which ships with iOS and macOS; other systems fall back to Jost) scaled to be exactly as wide as the date, then the temperature. The weather is not named or drawn in the header; the scene shows it. The three events start just right of this narrow column and run to the right edge; their text is 11 points with 12-hour times (AM and PM). The date block is spread to the same height as the events' text: its top is level with the first event's text and the temperature's bottom with the last event's text, with the day name evenly spaced between.
- Article titles are set in a humanist serif, in sentence case and without letter-spacing (Iowan Old Style, then Charter; both ship with iOS and macOS). Event titles, like everything else, keep the spaced capitals in Jost.
- The four buttons (settings, list, saved, and `∀` for all publications) share one flat colour per screen, with no shadow or glow: warm yellow-orange by day; light yellow (#ffffcc) at sunrise; pale peach (#ffcdba) at sunset; bright aqua at night. Two things were tried and rejected by the user: a set of signal yellow and strong blue, and giving the list button its own darker colour at sunrise, sunset and moon set. Highlighted text is still warm by day and cool at night. `?nobtn` draws a frame without the buttons.
- The three right-hand buttons are in one column: settings above the horizon, list centred on it, saved below. Behind them is one rounded dark blue panel, a little over 40% see-through: a deep blue at the top, where it shows against the sky, falling to near black at the bottom, where it melts into the sea. It has a soft glow over its top edge (blue, except at sunset, where it is dark pink) and a soft shadow under its bottom edge, and the scene's film grain lies over it. A version with the panel wholly above the horizon and the gradient reversed was tried and rejected by the user.
- Everything at the left shares one left edge, that of the phone's clock (34 points in): the date, day name, temperature and the article titles.
- The history button is `∀`, the maths sign for "all" (an upside-down A), drawn twice as heavy as the other symbols. It sits in the lower right corner, centred under the column of three buttons, at the height of the gap below the last article title. It has no panel; it keeps a soft dark shadow, which does not show on the black there.
- The article titles have no numbers. They start at the left edge, 104 points below the horizon.
- By day the sky is blue: lightest along the horizon and falling quickly to near black a little above the top of the sun's path, so the header and events stay on dark. Stars stay visible through it. It is duller and greyer under cloud and rain. Sunset is orange on the horizon with red and pink above, strongest on the sun's side. Sunrise is paler: yellow on the horizon, peach and a little pink above, then light blue. At night the sky is black as before.
- `index.html` is the animated mockup. Open it in a browser (`open design-ideas/v7-firmament/index.html`) and pick a state, or step through with the arrow keys. `?state=<id>&bare&still&t=<seconds>` draws one frame.
- `01-sunrise.png` … `12-moon-set.png` are stills of the same twelve states as `design-ideas/v1-original/`, at iPhone size.
- `fonts/` holds a copy of the app's Jost font so the page works offline.

Decided without being asked: the band of white light that sat inside the portal went with the portal; the arc is narrower than the screen so the setting sun clears the button column; the sea glints and the light on the water follow the sun or moon; by day the sea just under the horizon takes some of the sky's blue; the star field is new (spread over the whole sky), not v6's dust.
